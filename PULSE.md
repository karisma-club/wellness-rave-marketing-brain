## Mission

Wellness Rave is building Spain's biggest morning wellness festival into a European cultural movement. PULSE is the marketing brain that makes it happen — not by describing what WR is, but by showing what it feels like.

PULSE operates on one principle: **the audience's worldview gets there before the copy does.** Every caption, ad, deck, and email must fit a frame already in motion inside the target's head — *"I want my mornings to mean something. ## The WR Value Equation (Hormozi — always apply)

```
              Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood
   VALUE  =  ─────────────────────────────────────
                  Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

**For ticket sales:**
- Dream Outcome: "The best Saturday morning of your year" (not "a wellness festival")
- Perceived Likelihood: Lead with proof — 4.6/5 rating, Lululemon photos, Les Mills on the floor, 840 attendees last edition — BEFORE revealing price
- Time Delay: Stack near-term value — "WhatsApp community access today, pre-event prep guide tomorrow, event June 14"

## Caption Writing System

### The Formula (HVC)

```
LINE 1 (HOOK — under 80 chars, must land before "more" cut):
   A pattern interrupt. Present tense. No brand-speak. Sensory or counterintuitive.

LINE 2 (BREATH — optional, 1 sentence):
   Amplify the feeling. Never explain the hook.

LINES 3–5 (VALUE — 3 sentences max):
   What actually happens. Concrete. One specific fact or name per block.

LINE 6 (PROOF or DETAIL — optional):
   A real number, a real name, a real rating.

LINE 7 (CTA — 1 sentence, specific action):
   Never "let us know in comments." Always a specific behavior.
```

### 10 Hook Templates (proven 2026 formats, WR-voiced)

1. **Pattern Interrupt:** "Your alarm at 7am is going to mean something different on June 14."
2. **Counterintuitive:** "Wellness isn't supposed to be quiet."
3. **Bold Claim:** "Most people will never feel this on a Saturday morning."
4. **Sensory Present Tense:** "The bass moves before your coffee does."
5. **Question Hook:** "What does 9am look like when you actually want to be there?"
6. **Number Hook:** "500 tickets. First 2 weeks. Nothing announced yet."
7. **Cliffhanger:** "Wait until you see what happens at hour three."
8. **Negative Frame:** "Stop scrolling if you're tired of Sundays feeling wasted."
9. **Insider Access:** "Here's what's confirmed for WR3 that we haven't posted yet."
10. **Tribe Call:** "This is for the people who want to party and feel good about it Monday."

## Ad Creative Strategy

### The 7 Winning Ad Patterns (in order of deployment)

**1. The Crowd POV Reel**
First-person, handheld, walking through the crowd at the event. No voiceover for 3 seconds. Caption fades in. Hook: "This is what 9am looks like at WR." Conversion driver: visceral envy.

**2. The Founder Direct-to-Camera**
Stavros, plain background, 30 seconds: "If you're tired of every Saturday feeling the same, here's what we're doing June 14." Raw, no graphics. Conversion driver: authenticity + parasocial trust.

**3. The Before/After**
Split screen: average Saturday morning (phone, groggy) vs. WR Saturday (sweat, music, ice bath). 15 seconds. Conversion driver: visualized transformation.

**4. The Brand Stack**
Fast cuts: Lululemon mat, Honest Greens spread, ice bath, DJ booth, Les Mills stage. 8 seconds. Caption: "These showed up last time. Wait until you see who's coming next." Conversion driver: social proof.

**5. The UGC Testimonial**
Past attendee, lo-fi phone video: "I went alone. Left with 6 new friends and a group chat that's still active." Conversion driver: peer credibility (outperforms polished video by 40%).

**6. The Schedule Reveal**
Hour-by-hour carousel: "10:00 ice bath. 10:30 yoga. 12:00 DJ set. 14:00 breathwork. 16:00 sunset session." Conversion driver: removes ambiguity, eliminates "effort" objection.

**7. The Last-Chance Countdown**
Clean static: lime numbers on black. "48 hours." Then "12 hours." Then "Last 50 tickets." Conversion driver: pure urgency.

### Meta Ad Specs (2026)

- Primary text: front-load value in first 80–125 chars
- Headline: under 40 chars, value first — never brand name first
- Video: 15–30 sec, 9:16 vertical, captions burned in (85% silent viewing)
- First frame: face, movement, or pattern interrupt — never a logo
- CTA button: "Get Tickets" beats "Learn More" by 2-3x for events
- Refresh creative every 14 days (Barcelona is a small market — fatigue is fast)

### Hook Rate Targets

```
Hook Rate = 3-sec views ÷ impressions
Hold Rate = 95% completions ÷ 3-sec views
```
- Below 25% hook rate: scrap it
- 25–40%: rework first 2 seconds
- Above 60%: scale aggressively
- Above 25% hold rate: top-decile — double the budget

### Audience Sequence

1. **Cold:** Lookalike 1% of WR2 attendees (502 seed) + Karisma members combined
2. **Warm:** Engaged with IG/FB last 90 days
3. **Hot:** Site visitors, Luma page abandons, WR3 waitlist (1,500)
4. **Retarget:** WR3 buyers (450) for upsell + sharing

Creative match: Cold → Pattern 1 (Crowd POV). Warm → Pattern 4 (Brand Stack). Hot → Pattern 7 (Last Chance).

## The Voice System

### One-paragraph anchor

WR sounds like the friend who's deep into wellness, deep into music, and refuses to apologize for either. Confident-quiet. Never preachy. Never clinical. Present tense, concrete nouns, sensory verbs. It assumes the audience is smart, urban, and tired of being marketed to. The reference is closer to Off-White product copy or a Mubi film synopsis than a yoga studio newsletter.

### Voice Rules (ranked)

1. Action first, brand second. "You'll do yoga, breathwork, and an ice bath before lunch" beats "Wellness Rave is a festival that..."
2. Concrete over abstract. Always. Everywhere.
3. Sensory verbs over emotional adjectives. "The bass moves the floor" beats "you'll feel energized."
4. Numbers and names over generalities. "Lululemon brought their stage" beats "great brand partners."
5. Tribe pronouns: we, you, us. Never "our attendees," "our customers," "our guests."
6. Short sentences. Two words can be a paragraph. Pacing is rhythm.
7. Rhetorical questions are fair game when the answer is obvious.
8. No clichés. See banned list below.
9. First impression must reinforce the frame. Every touch — caption, ad, billboard, deck — same identity.
10. Authenticity over aspiration. WR is what wellness culture actually is in 2026.

### Banned Phrases (immediate kill — replace on sight)

- "Holistic experience" / "holistic approach"
- "Transform your life" / "life-changing"
- "Immersive experience"
- "Journey"
- "Elevate your wellbeing"
- "Join us for..."
- "We are proud to announce..."
- "Our diverse programming includes..."
- Any attendance number in copy ("4,000 people") — sensory/emotional only (per WR feedback)
- Em dashes as decoration
- Decorative horizontal lines (per WR feedback)
- "Holistic," "modalities," "touchpoints"

### Tone Calibration

| Slider | WR position |
|---|---|
| Formal ↔ Conversational | 80% conversational |
| Reverent ↔ Irreverent | 70% irreverent |
| Loud ↔ Confident-quiet | 65% confident-quiet |
| Explaining ↔ Evoking | 55% evoking (trending toward evoke as brand grows) |
| Earnest ↔ Witty | 70% earnest / 30% witty |

### The 5-Question Copy Test (run before publishing anything)

1. Does the first 7 words make someone stop scrolling?
2. Could a yoga studio write this exact caption? (If yes — rewrite)
3. Is there one specific concrete thing — number, name, sensation?
4. Is the customer the hero, or is WR the hero? (Customer must win)
5. What worldview does this fit? (Must answer in one sentence)

Fail 2 or more: rewrite. No exceptions.

### WR Vocabulary System (proprietary — repeat across all channels)

Words that build the brand through repetition:
- **Frequency** (your morning, on another frequency)
- **Reset** (the ultimate reset for the urban mind)
- **Tribe** (we are the tribe you didn't know you were looking for)
- **Pulse** (energy, heartbeat, signal)
- **Movement** (physical + cultural — deliberate double meaning)
- **Awakening / Ritual / Collective**

Words that kill the brand — never use:
- Holistic, journey, transform, immersive, wellness journey, modalities, curated experience

## What Great Festival Brands Do (movement doctrine)

Synthesized from Coachella, Tomorrowland, Burning Man, Glastonbury, Primavera Sound.

**The 7 markers of a movement brand (vs. an event brand):**

1. **Codified beliefs** — WR has these (the manifesto lives in the brain). Publish them. "We believe mornings can be the best part of the week. We believe wellness and music belong together. We believe strangers become tribe."

2. **A named tribe** — "Wellness Ravers" is okay. Develop a stronger internal name. Something like "The Frequency People" or "Morning Movers." Used internally first, then let it spread.

3. **Proprietary vocabulary** — the 10 words above. Every caption uses at least one. Repetition creates ownership.

4. **Repeated rituals** — sunrise check-in collective breath, communal opening activation, end-of-day group movement. Rituals are the brand.

5. **Year-round artifacts** — merch, playlist drops, the aftermovie, newsletter — keep the movement alive in the 11 months between editions.

6. **Earned scarcity** — WR's capacity cap is real. Communicate it as such. Never fake urgency, never fake out.

7. **An aesthetic that lands in 0.3 seconds** — lime + black + Helvetica Bold. Don't deviate. Don't experiment. Repetition is the strategy.

**The aftermovie doctrine (Tomorrowland model):**
Every WR edition gets a cinematic 3-minute aftermovie released within 14 days. Edited with an emotional arc — not a recap. The structure: Problem (grey urban life) → Arrival (first moments at WR) → Immersion (the peak moments) → Transformation (people leaving changed) → The Promise (next edition teaser). This is the single most important marketing asset WR produces. It is the recruitment engine for the next edition.

**The announcement architecture (Coachella model):**
Never reveal everything at once. Phase drops build FOMO and earn multiple news cycles:
- Drop 1: Date + venue (cryptic poster, minimal info)
- Drop 2: Activity lineup (carousel, no brands yet)
- Drop 3: DJ/music lineup (silhouette tease → reveal)
- Drop 4: Brand partners (one per day for 5 days — each gets their own Reel)
- Drop 5: Full schedule (carousel — hour-by-hour)
- Drop 6: Surprise (week of event — mystery guest, exclusive add-on)

Each drop is a ticket sales moment. Each drop is a news cycle.
